# 12 — Nonlinear symmetry: the declared-generator view

`docs/09` §2 decided the **linear** case via $\mathfrak a(L) \subset \mathfrak{gl}(n)$,
and a later hardening pass showed that $\mathfrak a(L) = 0$ does *not* mean "no
symmetry" ($L = y - x^3$ has a quadratic one). This note settles the nonlinear
case. The conclusion is clean: **the intrinsic question is ill-posed, and there
is a rank-one theorem.**

## 1. Generators, and the two symmetry spaces

Fix a finite-dimensional space $\mathcal X$ of vector fields on $\Theta$ —
*linear*, *affine*, or *polynomials of degree $\le d$*. Define the **declared
symmetry space**

$$\mathfrak a_{\mathcal X}(L) \;=\; \{\, X \in \mathcal X \;:\; DL(\theta)\,X(\theta) = 0
\ \text{ for all } \theta \,\} \;=\; \mathcal X \cap \mathfrak s(L),$$

where $\mathfrak s(L) = \{X : DL\cdot X = 0\}$ is the **full** symmetry
pseudoalgebra — the sections of $D = \ker DL$, which is infinite-dimensional. The
fiber $F$ is a local orbit of the *pseudogroup* generated by
$\mathfrak a_{\mathcal X}(L)$ iff $\dim\{X(\theta) : X \in \mathfrak a_{\mathcal X}(L)\}
= \dim F$; it is a local orbit of a *finite-dimensional Lie group* iff moreover
$\mathfrak a_{\mathcal X}(L)$ closes under bracket with constant structure
(`docs/09` §2 Remark).

## 2. The intrinsic question is ill-posed (computable)

For polynomials of degree $\le d$, $\mathfrak a_{\mathcal X}(L)$ is the
degree-$\le d$ **section space** of $D$. For a rank-one $L : \mathbb R^2 \to \mathbb R$
it has dimension $d(d+1)/2$ — the triangular numbers — verified exactly:

```
symmetry_growth() == [(1,1), (2,3), (3,6), (4,10)]
```

It grows without bound. So the intrinsic symmetry algebra is infinite-dimensional
and "is this ambiguity a symmetry?" has no finite answer without a declared
$\mathcal X$. Symmetry is **group- and degree-relative**.

## 3. Rank-one theorem

**Theorem 3.1.** Let $L : \mathbb R^2 \to \mathbb R$ be of rank one ($DL \neq 0$).
Then $X = (L_y,\, -L_x)$ satisfies

$$DL \cdot X = L_x L_y - L_y L_x = 0, \qquad X(\theta) \neq 0,$$

so $\{X(\theta)\}$ has dimension $1 = \dim F$: **every rank-one fiber is a
1-parameter group orbit** — the flow of $X$ (of degree $\deg L - 1$).

*Consequence.* No rank-one ambiguity is "accidental." $\mathfrak a(L) = 0$ says
only that the **minimal symmetry degree is $\ge 2$**. Instances: $L = x^3 + y^3$
and $L = y - x^3$ have $\mathfrak a(L) = 0$ but a *quadratic* symmetry
(`polynomial_symmetry_dim` at $d = 2$ is $1$).

## 4. Codimension $\ge 2$: the foliation case

For $\operatorname{rank} D \ge 2$ the fiber can fail to be a finite-dimensional
group orbit. The criterion is that $D$ be a **Lie foliation** — a frame with
**constant** structure functions (Molino). Otherwise the fiber is transitive only
under the infinite-dimensional pseudogroup: a genuine **foliation**, with the
*holonomy* (`docs/10` §3) as the local-to-global obstruction. So the
"no finite-dimensional symmetry" kind is a **codimension $\ge 2$** phenomenon,
realized by foliations with nonvanishing Godbillon–Vey class.

*Explicit exact witness:* left open (a parametric non-Lie foliation on $\mathbb R^3$
with exact rational structure). Recorded as an open item.

## 5. The corrected trichotomy

`docs/09` §2's $\{$continuous, discrete, accidental$\}$ becomes, exactly:

| kind | condition |
|---|---|
| **continuous** | a finite-dim transitive symmetry exists ($\mathfrak a_{\mathcal X}$ spans $F$); $d = 1$ is the linear case |
| **discrete** | the fiber is an isolated finite orbit |
| **foliation** | no finite-dim transitive symmetry — **only for codim $\ge 2$** |

The word "accidental" is retired: every fiber of a polynomial law map is a
(polynomial) orbit; the only questions are the **minimal degree** $d$ and the
dimension of the group.

## 6. Status and open

Known: the section-space picture and Lie foliations (Molino); the rank-one theorem
is elementary. New here: the **declared-generator framing**, the **symmetry-degree
gradation**, and the **codimension-$\ge 2$ scoping** of the foliation case. Open:
an explicit exact non-Lie-foliation witness; a criterion for the *minimal*
symmetry degree; whether the foliation case is decidable from finitely many
derivatives.
